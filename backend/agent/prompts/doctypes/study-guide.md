Turn this material into a study guide — the document someone revises from the
night before, without the source to hand. Use exactly these sections.

> [!abstract] In one minute
> Three or four sentences placing the material: what it is about, and what a
> reader is expected to be able to do once they know it.

`## The spine` — a table of the concepts this material is built on:

| Concept | What it says | Where |
|---|---|---|

One row per concept, the middle column stating the thing itself (a definition,
an equation, a condition) rather than describing that it exists.

`## Worked examples` — two or three examples taken from the material, each
step on its own line saying both what was done and *why* it follows from the
step above. A list of steps with no reasons is a recipe, not an example. Omit
the section entirely if the material contains none.

`## Traps` — the errors this material invites, as callouts:

> [!warning] The condition people forget
> …

The condition dropped from a theorem, the two terms that get confused, the
step that looks skippable. Write one per trap the material names or implies,
and omit the section rather than inventing them.
